# Language scenario-theater patterns

Choose a situation where language is necessary to achieve an outcome, not merely the subject of a quiz. Useful patterns include resolving a mistaken order, clarifying travel disruption, negotiating responsibilities, handling a customer concern, joining a social exchange, conducting a short interview, or adapting a message for a different audience.

The best choice labels are complete, natural utterances or messages in the target language. Two choices should differ in meaning, register, directness, clarity, or repair strategy while remaining plausible in context. Avoid grammar labels and translated explanations. For Japanese, keep furigana on new or hard kanji in dialogue and choice labels so beginners can read without a full translation.

Characters react to what the learner's words would communicate. Show misunderstanding through a natural reply or stalled task, then allow clarification, paraphrase, confirmation, or a more appropriate register to repair it. At higher levels, use implicit intent, cultural pragmatics, interruption, incomplete information, and changing audience expectations.

The theater supports contextual language use, but its fixed choices do not prove independent speaking or writing. Never describe completion as productive mastery.
